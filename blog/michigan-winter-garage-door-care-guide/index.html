A Michigan Winter Garage Door Care Guide for West Bloomfield Homeowners

Cold snaps are the single biggest driver of emergency garage door calls we take each year. A little prep in the fall goes a long way.

Every year, the coldest stretch of a Michigan winter brings a predictable spike in broken springs, frozen tracks, and openers that suddenly stop responding. Most of this is preventable with a bit of seasonal attention before the deep cold sets in.

Inspect Your Weather Seal Before the First Freeze

The rubber seal along the bottom of your garage door hardens and cracks over time, especially after a few Michigan winters. A cracked or gapped seal lets cold air, moisture, and even pests into the garage, which drives up heating costs and accelerates hardware corrosion. Homes near West Bloomfield's lakes see this faster than homes further inland, since the added humidity works against the rubber year-round. A weather seal replacement in the fall is one of the cheapest ways to protect everything else in your garage through the winter.

Lubricate Moving Parts, But Use the Right Product

Rollers, hinges, and the torsion spring itself benefit from a light coating of a silicone-based garage door lubricant before winter. Avoid WD-40 or heavy grease, which attract dirt and can gum up in cold temperatures. A quick pass with the right lubricant reduces the strain on your springs and opener during the coldest months, when metal parts are naturally stiffer and more brittle.

Watch for the January and February Spring Spike

Cold makes spring steel more brittle, and it's the single biggest cause of the emergency calls we take between January and March. Most of these springs weren't damaged by the cold itself, they were already worn down from a full year of normal use, and the cold snap was simply the final stress that caused them to let go. If your door is showing any of the warning signs of a failing spring, fall and early winter are the best time to address it, before temperatures drop further.

Keep Track Areas Clear of Ice and Snow Buildup

If your garage isn't heated, check the bottom of the tracks periodically during heavy snow or freezing rain. Ice buildup at the base of the track can prevent the door from seating properly and put extra strain on the opener as it tries to force the door shut against resistance it wasn't designed to fight.

Don't Ignore a Slow or Straining Opener

An opener that suddenly seems to take longer to open the door, or that strains and makes new noises in cold weather, is often telling you the springs are compensating for that extra effort. Rather than waiting for a full failure, a seasonal maintenance and tune-up before winter fully sets in can catch small issues while they're still inexpensive fixes.

The Bottom Line

Most winter garage door emergencies in West Bloomfield are years of gradual wear meeting one cold morning. A fall inspection covering the weather seal, lubrication, and a spring check catches the vast majority of issues before they turn into a door that won't open on the coldest day of the year.
