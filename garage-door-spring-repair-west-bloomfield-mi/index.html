If you heard a loud bang from the garage and now the door won't budge — or the opener is straining and groaning trying to lift it — you're almost certainly dealing with a broken spring. It's the single most common call we get from West Bloomfield homeowners, and it's genuinely not a DIY fix: garage door springs are under enormous tension and have seriously injured people who tried to replace them without the right training and tools.

Torsion Springs vs. Extension Springs

Most West Bloomfield homes use torsion springs mounted horizontally on a shaft above the door opening, which wind and unwind to counterbalance the door's weight. Older homes, and some lower-cost original installations from decades past, sometimes use extension springs running along the horizontal tracks instead. We carry both on the truck and will identify which one your door uses, assess why it failed, and explain the difference if you're curious.

Why Springs Fail in West Bloomfield Specifically

Garage door springs are rated for a set number of open-close cycles, typically 10,000 to 15,000 depending on quality. Michigan's cold winters make steel more brittle and accelerate that fatigue process, which is a large part of why so many West Bloomfield springs seem to fail specifically in January and February even though the wear happened gradually across the whole year. Add to that the township's lake geography — homes near Cass Lake, Pine Lake, Orchard Lake, and Walnut Lake see more humidity exposure, which can accelerate rust on spring coils and shorten their effective service life beyond what cycle count alone would predict. If your spring is more than 7 to 10 years old, it's genuinely worth having it inspected before it fails, particularly if you're closer to the water.

Signs Your Spring Is About to Fail

A spring rarely fails with zero warning. Watch for a door that seems to be getting heavier to lift manually, an opener working noticeably harder or louder than it used to, visible gaps or rust in the spring coils, or a door that doesn't hold its position when partially open — a properly tensioned door should stay in place at any height without the opener's help.

Our Spring Replacement Process

We inspect both springs, even if only one has broken. The second spring is usually close behind on cycle count, and mismatched spring age between the two sides is a common cause of doors that hang crooked or wear unevenly even after a single-side repair.

We measure your door's exact weight and dimensions. This matters even more for West Bloomfield's higher share of custom and insulated doors, where an off-the-shelf spring rating often won't be correct — using the wrong spring is both a safety issue and a common cause of premature opener wear.

We replace and properly balance the door. A correctly tensioned door should hold its position at any height without opener assistance — this is the test we use to confirm the job was done right.

We test full-cycle operation before we leave. Multiple complete cycles, checking for smooth, even, quiet operation on both sides.

What Spring Replacement Typically Costs

Most single-spring replacements in West Bloomfield fall within a predictable, moderate price range, with custom or heavier insulated doors requiring higher-rated springs at a somewhat higher cost. We'll confirm your exact quote after inspection and before starting any work. If both springs need replacement — common given how closely matched their wear typically is — we'll explain that clearly rather than surprising you partway through the job.

Frequently Asked Spring Replacement Questions

Why did my spring break in the winter when it worked fine all year? Cold weather makes steel more brittle, which accelerates the failure of a spring that's already been gradually wearing out through normal use. It's a seasonal pattern, not a coincidence.

Does living near a lake actually affect my spring's lifespan? Yes — the added humidity near Cass Lake, Pine Lake, Orchard Lake, and Walnut Lake can accelerate rust and corrosion on spring coils, which is worth factoring into your maintenance schedule if you're close to the water.

Do I need to replace both springs, or just the broken one? In most cases we recommend replacing both, since the second spring is usually close behind on wear even if it hasn't failed yet.

Is it dangerous to try opening the door manually with a broken spring? Yes — the door becomes extremely heavy and difficult to control safely without the spring's counterbalance.

How long does spring replacement typically take? Most single or dual spring replacements are completed within an hour or two once the technician is on-site, and are typically finished same-day from the initial call.
