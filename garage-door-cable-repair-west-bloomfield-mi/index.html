If your garage door is hanging crooked, sagging noticeably on one side, or you can see a cable that's visibly snapped and dangling near the bottom bracket, it needs attention before you try to operate the door again. Garage door cables work under significant tension alongside the springs, and a failed cable can let a door drop unevenly, come off track entirely, or in some cases fall in a way that poses a real safety risk.

Signs Your Cable Needs Repair

Door hangs noticeably lower on one side. Often the first visible sign of a cable problem, sometimes before the cable has fully failed.

A frayed or visibly damaged cable near the bottom roller bracket. Cables typically show wear before they snap — individual strands fraying, visible rust, or thinning at stress points.

A loud snapping sound followed by the door tilting to one side. Similar to a spring failure, but usually accompanied by uneven movement rather than a full refusal to move.

The door reverses or jerks partway through opening. Sometimes a symptom of a cable that's slipped off its drum or is beginning to bind.

Common Causes We See in West Bloomfield

Cable wear accelerates with age, rust, and a lack of routine lubrication — a real factor in some of the township's older homes where original cables have never been serviced. West Bloomfield's lake geography adds a meaningful factor here too: homes near Cass Lake, Pine Lake, Orchard Lake, and Walnut Lake see accelerated cable and drum corrosion from ambient humidity compared to homes further from the water, which is part of why we see a slightly higher rate of cable-related calls from lakefront and near-lake properties.

Our Repair Process

We assess both cables, not just the failed one. Cables typically wear at a similar rate on both sides, and replacing only the failed cable is a common cause of the door hanging crooked again within months.

We replace both cables as a pair when warranted. Not an automatic upsell — if one cable is significantly newer or shows meaningfully less wear, we'll explain that and recommend accordingly.

We re-tension the full system. Cables and springs work together, and after replacement we confirm the door is properly balanced across its full range of motion.

We run a complete operational test. Multiple full cycles, checking for smooth, even movement with no jerking, binding, or uneven tension on either side.

What Cable Repair Typically Costs

Cable repair is generally one of the more affordable garage door fixes we handle, and most jobs — including replacing both cables as a pair — are completed in under an hour once a technician is on-site.

Why This Isn't a DIY Job

Like springs, garage door cables operate under real tension as part of a balanced system, and attempting to replace or adjust them without the proper tools carries genuine injury risk. This is squarely in the category of repairs we'd recommend always leaving to a trained technician.

Frequently Asked Cable Repair Questions

Is a sagging door dangerous to operate? We'd recommend not operating the door until it's been inspected — a compromised cable can fail further under continued use.

Why do you recommend replacing both cables even if only one failed? Cables generally wear at a similar rate on both sides, and pairing the replacement helps ensure even, balanced operation going forward.

Does living near one of West Bloomfield's lakes really affect cable wear? Yes, the added humidity near the water accelerates corrosion on cables and hardware more than it does for homes further inland.

How long does cable repair take? Most jobs are completed in under an hour once a technician is on-site, with same-day service the norm.
